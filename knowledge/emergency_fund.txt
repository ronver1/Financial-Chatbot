An emergency fund is money set aside to cover unexpected expenses such as job loss, medical bills, or urgent home and car repairs. A common guideline is to save three to six months of essential living expenses, where essential means rent or mortgage, utilities, food, insurance, and minimum debt payments rather than discretionary spending. People with variable income, a single income supporting a household, or less job security often aim for the higher end of that range or beyond. The fund should be kept in a liquid, low-risk place such as a high-yield savings account so it is available immediately, and it should not be invested in stocks, because a market downturn could shrink it exactly when it is needed. Building even one month of expenses first is a meaningful milestone before working toward the full target.
