Roth IRA contributions are subject to income phase-outs based on modified adjusted gross income and filing status. For tax year 2026, the ability to contribute to a Roth IRA phases out between $153,000 and $168,000 of modified adjusted gross income for single filers and heads of household, and between $242,000 and $252,000 for married couples filing jointly. Below the lower end of the range you can contribute the full amount; within the range your allowed contribution is reduced; above the upper end you cannot contribute directly, though a backdoor Roth conversion may still be available. These thresholds affect Roth IRAs specifically and do not limit contributions to a traditional IRA, although income can affect whether traditional IRA contributions are tax-deductible. Note: these figures apply to tax year 2026 and are adjusted annually for inflation, so they should be verified against IRS.gov each year.
