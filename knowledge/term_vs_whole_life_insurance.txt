Term life insurance and whole life insurance serve different purposes. Term life covers a fixed period, such as ten, twenty, or thirty years, and pays out only if the insured dies during that term. It has no investment component, which makes premiums relatively low, and it is typically used to cover a temporary need such as replacing income while children are dependent or while a mortgage is being paid off. Whole life insurance is permanent coverage that lasts for the insured's lifetime and includes a cash value component that grows over time and can be borrowed against. Its premiums are substantially higher for the same death benefit. For most people whose main goal is income replacement during working years, term life provides the needed protection at far lower cost, and separately investing the difference is often more efficient than the savings built into whole life.
