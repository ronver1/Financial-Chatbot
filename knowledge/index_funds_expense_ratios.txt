An index fund is a mutual fund or exchange-traded fund that aims to match the performance of a market index, such as a broad stock market index, rather than trying to beat it through active stock picking. Because it simply holds the securities in the index, it requires little active management and therefore charges low fees. That fee is expressed as the expense ratio, an annual percentage of assets deducted from the fund; for example, an expense ratio of 0.05 percent means five dollars per year on every ten thousand dollars invested. Small differences in expense ratios compound significantly over decades, so lower-cost funds tend to leave investors with more money over the long run. Broad, low-cost index funds are widely recommended for long-term investors because they provide diversification and low fees without requiring the investor to select individual stocks.
