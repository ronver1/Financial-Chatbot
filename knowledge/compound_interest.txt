Compound interest is interest earned not only on the original principal but also on the interest that has already accumulated, so the balance grows at an accelerating rate over time. Because growth builds on prior growth, the length of time money stays invested matters more than the size of any single contribution. This is why starting early is so powerful: a smaller amount invested in your twenties can outgrow a larger amount invested in your forties, simply because it has more years to compound. The same principle works against you with debt, where unpaid interest on credit cards or loans compounds and makes balances grow quickly. A rough shortcut is the Rule of 72: dividing 72 by an annual growth rate estimates how many years it takes for money to double at that rate.
