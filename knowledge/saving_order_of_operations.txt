A widely used order of operations for allocating money helps prioritize when funds are limited. First, build a small starter emergency fund of about one month of expenses to avoid going into debt over minor surprises. Second, contribute to an employer retirement plan at least up to the full employer match, since an unmatched match is effectively leaving free money on the table. Third, pay off high-interest debt such as credit cards, because their interest rate usually exceeds any expected investment return. Fourth, build the emergency fund up to a full three to six months of expenses. Fifth, contribute to tax-advantaged accounts such as an IRA or health savings account and increase retirement contributions toward the annual limits. Later steps include paying down lower-interest debt and investing in taxable brokerage accounts. This sequence is a general guideline and individual circumstances can justify reordering.
