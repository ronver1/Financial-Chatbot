Roth and traditional retirement accounts differ mainly in when you pay tax. Traditional 401(k) and traditional IRA contributions are typically made with pre-tax dollars, lowering taxable income in the year of contribution, but withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income. Roth 401(k) and Roth IRA contributions are made with after-tax dollars and provide no deduction now, but qualified withdrawals in retirement, including investment growth, are tax-free. The core decision is whether you expect your tax rate to be higher now or in retirement: if you expect a higher rate later, Roth is generally more favorable; if you expect a lower rate later, traditional may be. Younger savers early in their careers, who are often in lower tax brackets, frequently favor Roth. Many people split contributions across both to diversify their future tax exposure.
