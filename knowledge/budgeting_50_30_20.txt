The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting framework that divides after-tax income into three buckets. Fifty percent goes to needs, which are essential expenses such as housing, utilities, groceries, transportation, insurance, and minimum debt payments. Thirty percent goes to wants, meaning discretionary spending like dining out, entertainment, travel, and hobbies. The remaining twenty percent goes to savings and debt paydown beyond the minimums, including emergency savings, retirement contributions, and extra payments toward high-interest debt. The percentages are guidelines rather than strict rules and can be adjusted for high cost-of-living areas or aggressive savings goals. Its value is simplicity: it gives a quick structure for people who have never budgeted, without requiring them to track every category in detail.
